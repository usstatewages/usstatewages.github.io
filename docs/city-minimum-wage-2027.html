City and County Minimum Wage Increases, January 1, 2027

Updated October 28, 2026

Dozens of cities and counties set a minimum wage above their state's. When a local rate is higher, employers inside that city have to pay it. These are the local rates for January 1, 2027 that have been officially published so far. The highest is Seattle at $22.14 an hour.

Details and exceptions

  • Seattle: All employers. Announced by the Seattle Office of Labor Standards on September 28, 2026.
  • West Hollywood: Non-hotel employees; hotel workers have a separate rate that changes July 1.
  • Denver: Up 2.9% from 2026.
  • Petaluma: Workers aged 14-17 may be paid a learner's wage of $16.10 for their first 160 hours.
  • Hayward (26+ employees): Employers with 25 or fewer workers: $17.40, the state rate.
  • Boulder (city): City of Boulder ordinance.
  • Minneapolis: All employers, regardless of size or worker age.
  • Saint Paul (101+ employees): Employers with 6-100 workers stay at $16.37 until July 1, 2027, then rise to $17.02. Employers with 5 or fewer stay at $14.25, then rise to $15.00 on July 1, 2027.

Other places with a local minimum wage

  • Washington: SeaTac, Tukwila, Renton, Bellingham, Everett, Burien, and unincorporated King County also set higher local rates.
  • Colorado: Edgewater and unincorporated Boulder County also have local minimum wage ordinances; their 2027 rates hadn't been finalized in official sources when this page was last checked.
  • Arizona: Flagstaff and Tucson set their own, higher minimum wages, which also change on January 1.
  • California: More than 30 other California cities and counties have their own minimum wage, and most adjust on January 1. Los Angeles, San Francisco, and Berkeley adjust on July 1 instead.
  • Maine: Portland, Maine sets its own minimum wage by city ordinance.

This list grows as cities publish their 2027 rates. Local rates aren't added until the city, or an employer-compliance source that matches it, publishes the actual figure.
